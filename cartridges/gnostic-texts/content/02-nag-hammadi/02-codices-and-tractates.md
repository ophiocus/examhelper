# The Codices and Their Tractates

The Nag Hammadi codices are not a single anthology assembled by one hand. Each codex reflects the interests of its compiler. Some lean Sethian, some Valentinian, some Hermetic; one contains a fragment of Plato's *Republic* in a garbled Coptic rendering.

## Codex I (the Jung Codex)

Purchased separately and presented to the Jung Institute in Zurich in 1952, later returned to Cairo.

- *Prayer of the Apostle Paul*
- *Apocryphon of James*
- *Gospel of Truth* (Valentinian)
- *Treatise on the Resurrection*
- *Tripartite Tractate* (the longest Valentinian system preserved in a gnostic voice)

## Codex II

The best-preserved codex; the leather binding survives intact.

- *Apocryphon of John* (long recension)
- *Gospel of Thomas*
- *Gospel of Philip*
- *Hypostasis of the Archons*
- *On the Origin of the World*
- *Exegesis on the Soul*
- *Book of Thomas the Contender*

## Codex III

- *Apocryphon of John* (short recension)
- *Gospel of the Egyptians* (Sethian, not the heresiological *Gospel of the Egyptians*)
- *Eugnostos the Blessed*
- *Sophia of Jesus Christ* (a Christianized rewrite of *Eugnostos*)
- *Dialogue of the Savior*

## Codex IV

Contains a second copy of the *Apocryphon of John* (long) and a second *Gospel of the Egyptians*.

## Codex V

- *Apocalypse of Paul*
- *First Apocalypse of James*
- *Second Apocalypse of James*
- *Apocalypse of Adam*

## Codex VI

The strangest of the codices; combines Christian, Hermetic, and Platonic material.

- *Acts of Peter and the Twelve Apostles*
- *Thunder, Perfect Mind* (a startling first-person revelation poem)
- *Authoritative Teaching*
- *Concept of Our Great Power*
- Excerpt from Plato's *Republic* 588b–589b
- *Discourse on the Eighth and Ninth* (Hermetic)
- *Prayer of Thanksgiving* (Hermetic)
- Excerpt from the *Asclepius* 21–29 (Hermetic)

## Shape of the corpus

Roughly half the tractates are **Sethian** (the *Apocryphon of John*, *Hypostasis of the Archons*, *Zostrianos*, *Allogenes*, *Three Steles of Seth*, *Trimorphic Protennoia*, *Marsanes*, *Gospel of the Egyptians*). Another substantial group is **Valentinian** (the *Gospel of Truth*, *Tripartite Tractate*, *Interpretation of Knowledge*, the *Valentinian Exposition*, the *Gospel of Philip*). A third is **Thomasine** (*Gospel of Thomas*, *Book of Thomas*). A fourth is **Hermetic**. Several tractates resist classification.

The library's plurality is itself the lesson: fourth-century Egyptian readers of these texts did not view Christianity, Platonism, Hermetism, and Sethian myth as mutually exclusive.
