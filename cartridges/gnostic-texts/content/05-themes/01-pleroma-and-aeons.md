# Pleroma and Aeons

## The vocabulary

**Pleroma** (Greek *plērōma*, "fullness", "completion") is the gnostic term for the divine realm in its integrity — the totality of aeons surrounding the ineffable Father. The word is drawn from Paul's letters ("In Christ the whole *plērōma* of deity dwells bodily", Col. 2:9), and gnostic writers both quote Paul and extend his usage into a technical cosmological term.

**Aeon** (*aiōn*) in ordinary Greek means "age" or "lifespan"; in gnostic usage it denotes a **divine emanation**, a hypostatized aspect of the Father existing as a personal being within the Pleroma. Aeons are not stages of time but inhabitants of the divine world, arranged in pairs, triads, and higher numerical groupings.

## The Valentinian Pleroma

The Valentinian system, as reported by Irenaeus (*Adv. Haer.* I.1) and confirmed by the *Tripartite Tractate* and the *Valentinian Exposition* at Nag Hammadi, counts **thirty aeons**, grouped in an Ogdoad, a Decad, and a Dodecad.

- **The Ogdoad (8).** Four pairs emanated first:
  - **Bythos** ("Depth") and **Sigē** ("Silence") — the Father and his consort.
  - **Nous** ("Mind") and **Alētheia** ("Truth").
  - **Logos** ("Word") and **Zōē** ("Life").
  - **Anthrōpos** ("Human") and **Ecclēsia** ("Assembly").
- **The Decad (10).** Five further pairs emanated by Logos and Zōē.
- **The Dodecad (12).** Six further pairs emanated by Anthrōpos and Ecclēsia. The youngest and last is **Sophia** ("Wisdom"), whose passion and fall drive the cosmogony.

The thirty aeons together constitute the Pleroma. Their arrangement in pairs — male-female *syzygies* — means that each aeon has a consort; an aeon alone is an aeon in dysfunction. Sophia's error is precisely to reach upward **without her consort**, producing formless offspring and a rupture in the fullness.

## The Sethian Pleroma

The Sethian texts (the *Apocryphon of John*, *Trimorphic Protennoia*, *Zostrianos*, *Allogenes*) give a different structure — less arithmetically neat than the Valentinian, more hierarchical.

- **The Invisible Spirit** — the unknowable Father.
- **Barbelo** — the First Thought, consort and mirror of the Invisible Spirit, described in litanies as *triple male*, *triple powerful*, *triple named*, *triple androgynous*.
- **Autogenes** — the Self-Begotten, the divine Christ figure.
- **Four Luminaries** — Harmozel, Oroiael, Daveithe, and Eleleth — each presiding over a class of beings.
- **Seth** — the heavenly archetype of the earthly Seth, guardian of the seed of the elect.

Sophia in Sethian texts belongs to the fourth luminary Eleleth's region and is responsible, as in Valentinianism, for the rupture that produces the demiurge.

## Emanation, not creation

The Pleroma does not come into being by fiat. It emanates — an overflow of the Father into knowability, with each aeon both knowing the Father and being, as it were, an angle of the Father's self-knowledge. The language is deliberately non-creational. Creation, in Gnostic texts, is what the demiurge does **below** the Pleroma and **in ignorance** of it.

This distinction — emanation above, creation below — is one of the sharpest lines dividing gnostic cosmology from orthodox Christian and rabbinic doctrine. For orthodox readers, the God of Genesis is the Pleroma itself speaking; for gnostics, the God of Genesis is a lower power who does not know the Pleroma exists.

## The number thirteen

The *Pistis Sophia* adds a characteristic detail: **Pistis Sophia dwelt in the thirteenth aeon** — above the twelve aeons of the rulers but just below the Treasury of Light. The thirteenth is consistently the border between the archontic cosmos and the kingdom of light. Sophia's fall is, structurally, a fall from thirteen into chaos; her restoration is a return to her thirteenth place.
