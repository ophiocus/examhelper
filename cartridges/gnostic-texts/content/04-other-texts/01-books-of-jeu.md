# The Books of Jeu (Bruce Codex)

## The manuscript

The **Bruce Codex** (Bodleian Library, MS Bruce 96) was brought to Europe by the Scottish traveler **James Bruce** in 1769, who purchased it at Thebes (modern Luxor). It lay essentially unstudied for over a century before Carl Schmidt produced the first critical edition in 1892.

The codex is a composite of papyrus leaves, in poor condition, in Sahidic Coptic. It contains:

- **The First Book of Jeu** and **the Second Book of Jeu** — sometimes bound together under the title *The Book of the Great Logos According to the Mystery*.
- An **untitled tractate**, often called the *Anonymous Treatise of the Bruce Codex*, which differs sharply in style and content from the Books of Jeu.

## The Books of Jeu

The two books are a **ritual manual disguised as a revelation dialogue**. The risen Christ addresses the disciples — again, as in the *Pistis Sophia*, Mary Magdalene is prominent among the questioners — and delivers:

- The **seals** and **names** and **numbers** of the aeons through which a soul must pass.
- Diagrammatic figures of the **emanations of the Treasury of Light**.
- A ritual of **baptism of water, baptism of fire, and baptism of the Holy Spirit**.
- Prescriptions for the preparation of the disciples (fasts, vestments, utterances) before the mysteries are transmitted.

**Jeu** himself is described as the **"first man"** — the figure set by the Father over all the emanations as their steward and the overseer of the Treasury. The two books catalogue the emanations by name and include drawn diagrams (surviving in the manuscript as crude cartouches with Coptic and Greek letter names).

Unlike the *Pistis Sophia*, which is primarily mythological with ritual references, the Books of Jeu are primarily **ritual**, with just enough mythology to frame the rites. They are closer to a priestly handbook than a catechism.

## Relation to the *Pistis Sophia*

The *Pistis Sophia*, at Book III chapter 99, makes an explicit cross-reference: the disciples ask about the mysteries, and Jesus replies, *"I have given them to you in the two books of Jeu, which Enoch wrote when I spoke with him out of the tree of knowledge and the tree of life, which were in the paradise of Adam."* This is almost certainly a reference to the Books of Jeu of the Bruce Codex, and it makes the Books of Jeu either contemporary with or older than the *Pistis Sophia*.

Carl Schmidt and the scholars who followed him have therefore treated the Books of Jeu as belonging to the same tradition and perhaps the same community as the *Pistis Sophia*, with the Books of Jeu functioning as the ritual substrate to which the *Pistis Sophia*'s mythology is the narrative accompaniment.

## The Anonymous Treatise

The third text in the Bruce Codex is a different work entirely. It is a **Sethian-style cosmogony** with no clear Christian frame. It describes the Father, his emanations, the Monogenes, a Triple-Powered Invisible Spirit, and a series of aeons and dwellings — vocabulary closely related to the *Allogenes* and *Zostrianos* of Nag Hammadi.

Scholars have sometimes called this treatise the *Untitled Gnostic Treatise* or, following J. D. Turner's proposal, have classed it as a **Sethian ascent apocalypse** parallel to the Platonizing Sethian texts of Nag Hammadi. It likely predates the Christianization of the Sethian material and gives a glimpse of a pre-Christian Sethian milieu.
