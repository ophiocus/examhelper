# The Berlin Codex (BG 8502)

## The manuscript

**Papyrus Berolinensis 8502**, usually called the **Berlin Codex** or simply **BG**, was purchased in Cairo in **January 1896** by the German scholar **Carl Reinhardt**, apparently from an antiquities dealer who claimed it had come from Akhmim in Upper Egypt. It was acquired by the Egyptian Museum in Berlin.

The codex is a small leather-bound papyrus book, 135 pages, Coptic in a Sahidic with Subakhmimic features, dated paleographically to the late fourth or early fifth century.

Its publication was delayed by an extraordinary series of misfortunes: a burst water pipe in the museum in 1912 damaged the working transcription, the First World War halted work, the chief editor Carl Schmidt died in 1938 with the edition still unfinished, and the Second World War intervened before Walter Till could complete it. The editio princeps finally appeared in **1955** — nearly sixty years after the manuscript reached Berlin.

## The four tractates

- **The Gospel of Mary** (pages 7–19)
- **The Apocryphon of John**, short recension (pages 19–77)
- **The Sophia of Jesus Christ** (pages 77–127)
- **The Act of Peter** (pages 128–141)

Three of these four are also preserved at Nag Hammadi: the *Apocryphon of John* in three copies (NHC II,1; III,1; IV,1), the *Sophia of Jesus Christ* in one (NHC III,4). Only the *Gospel of Mary* and the *Act of Peter* are unique to the Berlin Codex.

## The Gospel of Mary

This is the most famous text of the codex, and the only **gospel in the ancient world with a woman's name in the title**. Only about **eight of the original eighteen pages** survive; pages 1–6 and 11–14 are missing.

What survives breaks into two movements:

- **First section (extant pp. 7–9).** After a dialogue between the Savior and the disciples on the nature of matter and sin, the Savior departs. The disciples are afraid. **Mary stands up, embraces them, and turns their hearts toward the Good.** Peter asks Mary: *"Sister, we know that the Savior loved you more than the rest of women. Tell us the words of the Savior which you remember, which you know but we do not, nor have we heard them."*
- **Second section (extant pp. 10, 15–19).** Mary reports a vision she received, including a dialogue with the Savior on the ascent of the soul through four powers: **Darkness, Desire, Ignorance, and Wrath**. The soul gives an answer to each power and passes upward. When Mary finishes, **Andrew objects** that her teachings are strange, and **Peter objects** that the Savior would not have preferred a woman to the men. **Levi defends her**: "If the Savior made her worthy, who are you indeed to reject her?"

The confrontation between Peter and Mary in the *Gospel of Mary* belongs to the same traditional landscape as Peter's objection in *Pistis Sophia* ch. 36 and Jesus's saying at *Thomas* 114 — a pattern in which Peter opposes Mary's authority and the Savior or his representative upholds it.

## The Apocryphon of John (BG recension)

The Berlin Codex preserves the **short recension** of the *Apocryphon of John*. Nag Hammadi III,1 is the other short-recension copy; NHC II,1 and IV,1 are the long recension. The short recension lacks the extended Sophia material and the Pronoia hymn that conclude the long.

## The Sophia of Jesus Christ

A Christianized rewriting of the **non-Christian** Nag Hammadi tractate *Eugnostos the Blessed* (NHC III,3; V,1). The *Sophia of Jesus Christ* takes the philosophical cosmogony of *Eugnostos* and recasts it as a post-resurrection dialogue between the Savior and twelve disciples and seven women on a mountain in Galilee. The comparison of the two tractates is one of the clearest laboratory cases we possess of how a philosophical Hellenistic Jewish treatise could be reshaped into a Christian revelation dialogue.

## The Act of Peter

A short narrative of Peter healing his paralyzed daughter, then paralyzing her again to protect her chastity. Known also through the *Acts of Peter* tradition in Latin; its presence here is somewhat anomalous beside the three revelation dialogues.
