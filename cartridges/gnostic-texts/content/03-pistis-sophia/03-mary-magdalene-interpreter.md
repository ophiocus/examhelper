# Mary Magdalene as Interpreter (Chapters 17–20)

The stretch of chapters 17 through 20 of the *Pistis Sophia* shows the text's method at its clearest. In this sequence, Mary Magdalene steps forward three times in close succession to interpret what Jesus has just said. Reading these chapters in order, rather than jumping to the more famous cosmological questions of chapter 20, reveals how the dialogue is actually built.

## Chapter 17 — Jesus on the three powers he received

Jesus has just finished narrating how, as he descended through the regions, he passed among the rulers of the spheres and **took from them one-third of their power**, so that they would no longer be able to complete their evil works and so that souls could ascend. He also describes how he altered the **course of the fate-spheres** (*heimarmene*): instead of running continuously in the direction they had run from the beginning, he set them to turn six months to the left and six months to the right, so that the rulers could not know in advance the fate of the souls descending.

This is the speech that Mary, in chapters 18 and onward, will ask to interpret.

## Chapter 18 — Mary's interpretation

Mary Magdalene steps forward — not yet with a question, but with an **interpretation**. She connects Jesus's narrative about diminishing the archons' power to a prophecy in Isaiah 19:

> "Egypt, Egypt shall fight against Egypt, and the Egyptians shall fight against their brethren; city shall rise against city, and district against district."

Her reading: the "Egypt" of the prophecy is **matter** (*hylē*), and the civil war among Egyptians is the conflict that Jesus has now produced among the archons of matter by taking away one-third of their power. The archons who formerly operated in unison now strive against one another, and the harmony of fate is broken.

Jesus replies with one of his most emphatic commendations in the entire work:

> "Well done, Mary, thou blessed one, who shalt inherit the whole kingdom of the light."

It is important to note what this interpretation is doing. Mary is not solving a riddle; she is demonstrating the **gnostic method of reading prophecy**. A prophetic oracle about Egypt is not about Egypt — it is about the cosmic structure. This is the same hermeneutic principle at work in the Valentinian readings of Paul and in the Sethian rereadings of Genesis. The *Pistis Sophia* presents Mary as its exemplary practitioner.

## Chapter 19 — Further interpretation from Mary

Mary continues. She connects the diminishment of the archons' power to the word of Jesus to his disciples as recorded in the canonical gospels: **"I have given you power to tread on serpents and scorpions and on all the power of the enemy."** (Luke 10:19.)

The "serpents and scorpions" are the archons. The authority given to the disciples is the specific authority that Jesus has just described — the authority he obtained by taking a third of the archons' light-power. Mary's reading thus **ties the esoteric narrative to a canonical saying**, a gesture that recurs throughout the *Pistis Sophia* and that the text's compilers clearly want their readers to learn.

Again Jesus praises her: "Well done, Mary."

## Chapter 20 — Mary's first question on the spheres

Only now, after two interpretations that have established her authority, does Mary turn from interpreting to **questioning**. In chapter 20 she asks the question that will drive much of Book I:

> "My Lord, in what manner, if the rulers of the spheres are cast down by one another and their regions are no longer stable, in what manner will the men on earth be delivered from them?"

This opens the long exposition of **heimarmene** — the fate-spheres — which runs through the following chapters. Jesus will explain the mechanism of fate, how the archons draw up the pact that fixes each life at birth, how the alteration of the spheres' rotation disrupts that pact, and what this means for astrology, prayer, and the mysteries.

## Why the sequence matters

If a reader skips from chapter 17 directly to the cosmological material of chapter 20, it looks as though Mary is just a convenient interlocutor whose role is to ask useful questions. Reading 18 and 19 in place shows what the text is actually doing:

- Mary is introduced first as an **interpreter**, not a questioner.
- Her readings connect **prophetic scripture** and **canonical sayings** to the **esoteric narrative**.
- Only after she has demonstrated this hermeneutic authority does she receive the further revelation of the spheres.

The structure is pedagogical. The reader is being taught that the right to ask questions about the cosmos presupposes the capacity to read scripture gnostically. Mary's precedence among the disciples in the *Pistis Sophia* is not an accident of the narrative; it is the consequence of the hermeneutic she demonstrates in these chapters.
