# What Is Gnosticism?

**Gnosticism** (from the Greek *gnōsis*, "knowledge") names a family of religious and philosophical currents that flourished in the first centuries of the common era, principally in the eastern Mediterranean. It is not a single sect but a cluster of traditions that share a common diagnosis: salvation comes not through faith or ritual, but through **revealed knowledge** concerning the true origin and destiny of the soul.

## Core features

- **Radical dualism.** An essential distance separates the material world — fashioned defectively by an ignorant demiurge — from the *Pleroma*, the original divine fullness.
- **The divine spark.** The human being carries within a *pneuma* — a spark fallen from above, imprisoned in matter and in the cycles of fate (*heimarmene*).
- **Redemptive revelation.** An emissary (Christ, Seth, Barbelo, or Sophia, depending on the tradition) descends to remind the spark of its origin and to deliver the names, seals, and passwords that allow it to ascend through the spheres.
- **Rereading of scripture.** Gnostic texts reread Genesis, the Gospels, and the prophets through an inverted lens: the creator God of the Old Testament is, in many schools, the demiurge to be transcended.

## Principal schools

- **Valentinians.** Followers of Valentinus of Alexandria (2nd c.). A thirty-*aeon* emanation system; the fall and restoration of Sophia.
- **Sethians.** Tradition honoring Seth, son of Adam, as bearer of the spiritual seed. Key texts: *Apocryphon of John*, *Hypostasis of the Archons*, *Three Steles of Seth*.
- **Basilideans.** School of Basilides in Alexandria; cosmology of 365 heavens.
- **Thomasine Christianity.** Associated with the aphoristic *Gospel of Thomas*.
- **Mandaeans.** A surviving tradition in Iraq and Iran, centered on John the Baptist.
- **Manichaeans.** System of Mani (3rd c.) that spread from the Roman Empire to China; absolute light-darkness dualism.

## The problem of the sources

For centuries, Gnosticism was known almost exclusively through its adversaries: Christian **heresiologists** such as Irenaeus, Hippolytus, Tertullian, and Epiphanius, who quoted the gnostics in order to refute them. This filtered image changed radically with three codicological finds:

1. The **Askew Codex** (acquired in London, 1772), containing the *Pistis Sophia*.
2. The **Bruce Codex** (acquired in Egypt, 1769), containing the *Books of Jeu*.
3. The **Berlin Codex** (BG 8502, acquired in Cairo, 1896), containing the *Gospel of Mary* and other tracts.

To these was added, in 1945, the great find at **Nag Hammadi** (Egypt): thirteen codices with more than fifty tractates that transformed the study of early Christianity.
